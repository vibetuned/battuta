Playback in battuta is a checking tool, not a performance engine. It answers two
questions: *did I write the notes I meant?* and *does the form I notated actually
play that way?*

## The player

Switch to **page view** — the controls appear in the header's second row,
next to the score's title and tempo.

<Shot name="player" caption="Playing: pause, stop, the live speed select, the clickable progress bar and the elapsed/total readout — beside the score's title and ♩= tempo." />

| Control | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **▶ / ⏸** | Play or pause from the current position |
| **⏹** | Stop and clear the highlight |
| **speed** | 0.5×–2×, multiplying the score's own tempo — applies live, and is remembered |
| **progress bar** | Click anywhere to seek; the readout shows `elapsed / total` at the chosen speed |

The score's own tempo comes from the **♩=** button beside the title: it sets
the MEI tempo (`@midi.bpm`), page view prints the marking, and playback runs at
it (120 bpm when unset). The speed select multiplies on top.

The note being sounded lights up as it plays, and the view scrolls when the
playing measure leaves the window. Audio and highlight come from **one** timeline,
so they cannot drift apart.

<Shot name="player-highlight" caption="The sounding notes highlighted mid-playback in page view." />

Sound is a sampled piano — a subset of the Salamander piano bundled inside the
application. No network request, no CDN, nothing to configure.

<Aside type="caution" title="Any edit stops playback">
Editing the document, switching tabs, or switching views stops the player. The
timeline is computed from the document as it was, so the moment you change the
document it is stale — battuta stops rather than play something that no longer
matches what you see.
</Aside>

## What you hear reflects what you wrote

Playback reads the document, not just the pitches:

- **Ties are held.** A tie chain — including one that crosses barlines and one that
  spans repeated passes — sounds as a single attack for its full summed length.
- **Articulations shape the release.** Tenuto and notes under a slur or phrase mark
  sound their full value, staccato about half, staccatissimo about a third. An
  explicit articulation beats the slur it sits under.
- **Unmarked notes detach slightly** (about 90 % of their value), so legato is
  audible by contrast rather than being the default everywhere.
- **Accidentals, key signatures and measure accidentals** resolve to real sounding
  pitch, because Verovio resolves them before the note reaches the sampler.

## Repeats, voltas and jumps

The player takes the form: repeat barlines, volta sets, and one dal-segno or
da-capo jump with *fine* and *To Coda* respected. Repeated passes highlight the
notes you actually wrote, not copies of them.

The details of what is and isn't followed are on
[Repeats and form](../form/#playback-follows-the-form).

Everything the player performs — the expanded form, merged ties, the
articulation gates — can be written out as a file: **export playback MIDI** in
the battuta menu. See [Exporting](../files/#exporting).

## Limits worth knowing

- Playback lives in **page view only**. Edit view has no player.
- There is no selection-range playback yet, no metronome, and no per-staff
  mute. The score tempo is a single **♩=** for the whole piece — mid-score
  tempo changes are not performed yet.
- Dynamics and hairpins are notated but not yet performed as volume changes.
