On an iPad with an **Apple Pencil**, or an Android tablet with an **S Pen**,
the pen writes on the score in edit view. The **✎** item in the header,
beside the other plugins' buttons, switches between three ways of writing:

- **✎ cross** — notes, with a cross at the pen's tip;
- **✎ hand** — the symbols around them, written by hand;
- **✎ slur** — slurs and ties, drawn from note to note.

Every start is the cross. The pen writes **only on the score**: on the
toolbar, the panels, the on-screen keyboard or a menu it taps like a
finger. Fingers scroll and zoom as always, even while the pen writes.

<Aside type="note" title="Where it works">
On iPhone and iPad battuta needs iOS / iPadOS 18. On the desktop and in a
browser there is no pen to capture: the Stylus plugin does nothing there,
not even the ✎. It does not write in page view either, which has no
measures to write on.
</Aside>

## Notes with the cross

**Touch** a staff: a small cross comes up at the tip, with a ghost notehead
where the note will go — the pitch under the pen, at the input's length
(the one the digits 1–7 pick, dotted if you dotted it).

**Drag**, and past a few pixels the cross unfolds into two rulers that
cross like the words of a crossword:

- **across**, above the staff, a cell for every length from the 64th to
  the whole note, each where the pen has to be to choose it — drag right
  for a longer note, left for a shorter one; a dot stays;
- **down** through the note, a letter for every step of the staff at its
  height — the lines' letters on the left, the spaces' on the right — drag
  up and down for the pitch.

The choice is lit in both, the pitch with its octave, and pulses at every
change.

**Lift** the pen to write the note, over the note or rest nearest where it
landed on that staff, the way the keyboard enters a note: a shorter note
leaves rests for the rest of the time, a longer one takes the events after
it. A length you dragged to becomes the input's, as if you had typed the
digit. A **tap** is a lift where you touched: the note as the cross first
showed it. The key signature supplies the accidental, every note is one
undo step, and the caret goes to the note you wrote.

**Chords.** Squeeze the Apple Pencil Pro, or press the S Pen's side button,
then touch above or below a note: only the letters come up, and lifting
adds that pitch to the note — or to the chord it already is — at its own
length. A press arms just the next stroke; press again before touching to
disarm it.

**At the end of the score**, the gray measure with a **+** after the last
one adds a measure when you tap it with the pen (or a finger), so a score
grows without the keyboard. See [Structure](../structure/#measures).

## Symbols by hand

Tap **✎ cross**: it reads **✎ hand**. Write a symbol as you would on paper,
at its natural size, where it belongs — a sharp just left of its note, a
dot just right of it, a staccato above it, a fingering or a trill above the
staff, a dynamic below it, a clef on the staff, a note with its head where
the pitch is. Strokes that follow each other closely are one symbol: a
sharp's four strokes join, while a staccato written just after on the next
note is its own. The strokes show in blue as you write.

A moment after the last lift the symbol is read by **granita**, battuta's
own reader, which runs in the app and sends nothing anywhere. What it reads
is put in the score as the keyboard would put it, one undo step, and a
small label shows it **as it is engraved** — ♯, a note wearing its
staccato, **ff** — with how sure the reader was and a **✓ ✗** for three
seconds. Tap ✓, or nothing, and it stays; tap ✗ and it is undone and the
other readings are offered.

**When it isn't sure**, up to three readings appear beside the symbol, each
as engraved — only the ones that belong where you wrote: no fingering
inside the staff, no clef above it. Tap one to write it, or **none of
these** to discard the symbol.

**What it reads**: sharp, flat and natural; staccato, tenuto, accent,
staccatissimo and marcato; the augmentation dot; the fermata; fingerings 1
to 5; the trill, mordent and turn above a note, and the arpeggio just left
of a chord; the dynamics from **pp** to **fff**, **sfz**, **sf** and
**fz**; notes from the whole to the 16th; the G, F and C clefs; the coda
and segno signs, *Fine*, *D.S.* and *D.C.*

<Aside type="tip" title="The quarter rest erases">
A quarter rest is never written: draw one on a note and the note becomes a
rest of its length, as <kbd>Delete</kbd> does it; draw one on a fingering,
a dynamic, a fermata or an ornament and that mark goes. To remove an
accidental, an articulation or a dot, write the same mark again.
</Aside>

## Slurs and ties

Tap **✎ hand**: it reads **✎ slur**. Touch a note, or a chord, draw to
another note and lift on it: the two are **tied** when they are the same
note — the same written pitch, accidental included — and **slurred**
otherwise, in either direction. The bow is written as you lift, with the
✓ ✗ of handwriting: ✗ undoes it and offers the other kind.

## Replies and training

The reader learns from you, when you tell it. A reading you confirm with
**✓**, pick from the choices, or pick after a **✗** is a **reply**: the
symbol as you drew it and what it was. A reading you leave alone is not one.
Replies are kept on the tablet, in battuta's own data, and never sent
anywhere.

The Stylus row of battuta menu → 🌣 shortcuts → **plugins** shows how many
replies the tablet keeps and which reader is in use, with four buttons:

- **train** — retrains the reader on your replies, in well under a second,
  and reads with it from then on;
- **reset** — puts the released reader back; the replies stay;
- **share** — puts the replies on the share sheet, to contribute them to
  the next granita;
- **delete replies** — deletes them, after asking.

A new release of battuta's reader forgets what it learnt from you — it
learnt it on the old reader — but keeps your replies: train again.

## Turning it off

battuta menu → 🌣 shortcuts → **plugins** → *Stylus*. Off gives the pen back
to the page everywhere; the switch is remembered.
