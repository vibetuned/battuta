You are correcting a score against its source — an OMR transcription
against the scan it was read from, or your own transcription against an
edition. The PDF reference puts the source **above every measure** in
edit view: the scan on top, battuta's rendering below, so a wrong note,
a missing tie or a measure out of place shows at the measure, not three
windows away.

Click the **📄** in the header. A panel opens at the bottom.

## Loading a PDF

**load a PDF or images…** takes the PDF — or its pages as **images**
(PNG, JPEG, WebP…: one image a page, in the order a `_page1`, `_page2`… in
their names gives, else by name) — and its **annotation files**,
together: select them all at once. The annotation files are what
an OMR pipeline writes beside a PDF: one JSON per page, named
`…_page1.json`, `…_page2.json`…, boxing every measure and system on that
page. They are what tells battuta where each measure is. A piece's
`….graph.json` is used when no page file is given.

Every page is rendered once, in the editor — nothing is uploaded — and
the panel says how many of the score's measures it placed.

<Aside type="note" title="Which measure is which">
When your score carries the ids the annotations were made with — an OMR
output and its annotation files came out of the same pipeline — measures
are matched **by id**, exactly, whatever the order. Otherwise they are
matched **in order**: the score's first measure is the PDF's first, and
so on; if the two counts differ, the panel says both.
</Aside>

## Drawing systems and measures

A PDF without annotation files — a scan, an edition, any PDF — can be
placed by hand. Load the PDF alone and choose **draw systems and
measures**: the page opens in the panel, as wide as the window, and two
tools mark it.

- **▭ systems** — drag a box across a system, from its first clef to its
  last barline, top staff to bottom.
- **| barlines** — click in a system where a barline is: the system splits
  there. Click a barline again to take it away.
- **≡ staves** — click in a system between two staves to split it there;
  the staves are numbered down its left. Click a split again to take it
  away. With staves, the strip highlights the caret's staff inside its
  measure.

**−** and **+** zoom the page from the panel's width up to four times; what
was in the middle stays in the middle, and the page scrolls both ways.

Each system's measures are the stretches between its barlines, numbered as
the score's measures will be matched to them; the **×** at a system's
corner removes it. **◀ ▶** turn the pages, and the line beside them counts
what is drawn against the score's measures. The scans appear above the
tiles as you draw. **done** returns to the strip.

The drawing is kept for that PDF — load the same file again and it is
back — and it can be saved as annotation files: battuta menu → **export
PDF annotations**, one `…_pageN.json` per page, the same format the OMR
files come in.

## Above the measures

With **on the score** checked, every measure in edit view carries its scan
above it: the measure's width by the whole system's height, so every staff
is there, fitted to the tile so the barlines stand over each other. A
row's band is as tall as its tallest scan; uncheck the box and the room
goes with the scans.

## The panel

The strip at the bottom shows **the system the caret is in**, every
measure outlined and the caret's highlighted in amber — and, when the
system's staves are known (drawn, or `system-staff` boxes in the files),
the caret's staff in green; move the caret and the strip follows. **◀** and **▶** step through the systems, across pages.

## What it does not do yet

Nothing goes into your MEI: no `<facsimile>` or `<zone>` yet, and clicking
a scan does not move the caret. A drawing's measures are matched in
order — drawn by hand, they carry no ids. It is the first step of the facsimile, and those are the next ones.

## Turning it off

battuta menu → **🌣 shortcuts** → **plugins** → *PDF reference*. The 📄,
the panel and the scans go together, with the room above the measures.
Nothing about your score changes — the plugin only ever reads it.
