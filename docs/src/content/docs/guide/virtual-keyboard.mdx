Every battuta action is a keystroke — which is a problem on a tablet. The
on-screen keyboard puts the whole keymap on screen: toggle it with **🎹** in
the header (touch devices show it automatically), close it with its **×**.

<Shot name="vkeys" caption="Piano, octave rail, modifier latches, the digit pad, and one key per action, grouped like the shortcut editor." />

## The piano

Two octaves, playing into [note entry](../note-entry/) exactly like a MIDI
keyboard: in input mode a tap enters that pitch **at its written octave**
(black keys enter sharps), and **holding several keys builds a chord** — with
multi-touch, played like a real chord. The caret advances when the last
finger lifts.

The rail on the left steps the visible octaves (▲ / ▼ around the low octave's
label); a wheel or two-finger scroll over the piano does the same.

## Modifier latches

**ctrl**, **alt** and **shift** are *sticky*: tap one and it applies to the
next key, then releases — tap again to cancel. They behave like the real
modifiers, so everything in the [keyboard reference](../../reference/keyboard/)
holds: latch **shift** and **→** extends the selection, latch **alt** and the
arrows become shorten/lengthen duration.

While a latch is on, **the keys relabel to what they will now do**, and the
remapped ones are tinted:

<Shot name="vkeys-shift" caption="Shift latched: voltas on the digit pad, staccatissimo on the staccato key, tuplet on tie." />

## The digit pad

One pad, four meanings — the latches choose:

| Latch | 1–5 | 6–0 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *none* | duration (7 = whole … 1 = 64th, in input mode) | — |
| **shift** | volta 1–5 toggle (block selection) | volta 6–9 |
| **alt** | fingering 1–5 (add **shift** too to stack) | finger change → 1–5 |

## Everything else

The remaining groups mirror the keymap itself — the buttons are **generated
from your live bindings**, so a rebound key or a brand-new action shows up on
its own, and actions reached by a modifier (marcato = shifted accent,
measure repeat = shifted simile…) appear when their latch is on rather than
crowding the panel. The nav cluster is a D-pad (home/left, up/down,
end/right, paging), and the system group carries save, open, undo/redo,
copy/paste, zoom and the measure operations.

<Aside type="note" title="Also useful with a mouse">
Nothing about the panel is touch-only: it doubles as a clickable cheat sheet,
and the latch relabelling is a quick way to *discover* what shift or alt does
to a key before pressing it.
</Aside>
