You have the score and you have a recording of it — your own take, a
student's, a performance you are transcribing. The pitch reference puts
the two together: the recording's pitch is detected in the editor and
drawn over the notes, so the place where the take goes sharp, flat, early
or late is visible **at the note**, not on a tuner.

Click the **🎙** in the header. A panel opens at the bottom of the window.

## Loading a recording

**load a recording…** takes any audio file the browser can decode: wav,
mp3, ogg, flac, m4a. Nothing leaves your machine — the pitch is detected
in the editor, in the background, and the panel shows the progress. When
it is done a notice says how long the take is, how many frames had a
pitch and how far, in cents, the trace sits from the written pitch on
average — once, and then the panel is just the strip and its controls.

The recording is not a document: it opens no tab, is never saved with the
score, and is forgotten when you close battuta. What is remembered is the
alignment you settled on, per score (see below).

## The strip

<Shot name="pitch-reference" caption="The panel: the controls above, the whole recording below — waveform, measure ticks, the written notes in blue, the trace in yellow." />

The whole recording, left to right:

| What | Drawn as |
| --- | --- |
| the waveform | a grey band |
| each measure's start, through the alignment | a thin vertical tick |
| the written notes | blue bars — a bar per note, at its pitch, for its length |
| the recording's pitch | the yellow trace; a gap where nothing pitched was heard |
| where bar 1 begins | a dashed yellow line |
| the playhead | a white line: **▶** plays the recording from it, drag anywhere on the strip to move it |

Sing in tune and the trace runs along the blue bars. A note taken early
starts before its bar; a sharp one runs above it.

## Listening

Two transports start from the white line. **▶ song** plays the recording
and **⏸** pauses it where it is. **▶ score** plays the written notes
through every connected MIDI output — plain notes, at the recording's
pace, so the two can run together and you hear the take against the
reference pitch; without a MIDI output it says so. The line moves while
either plays. Drag anywhere on the strip to move it. Click a note in the
score and the line goes to that note's moment in the recording, through
the alignment — so the way to hear how you took a passage is to click
its first note and press play. Closing the panel stops both.

<Aside type="note" title="Plain notes on purpose">
*▶ score* is the reference pitch, nothing more: no piano, no ties, no
articulations, channel 1 at one velocity, and whatever your synth or DAW
has on that channel is what sounds. It plays in the take's register: the
**st** value is undone on the notes, so with the trace at +12 for a voice
an octave under the part, the reference sounds an octave under as well.
The notation's real performer is the [playback](../playback/) plugin, in
page view.
</Aside>

## Lining the two up

Two numbers put the recording on the score's clock, and a third puts it in
the score's octave:

**bar 1 at** — where the score begins in the recording, in milliseconds.
It is set for you at the first pitched sound. If the take had a count-in
or a breath, type where bar 1 really begins.

**st** — transpose the trace by that many semitones before it is compared
and drawn. A voice singing an octave below the written part is
−12; a transposing instrument, its interval. The detected pitch itself is
untouched, so the strip's yellow line moves and the recording does not.

**recorded at ♩=** — the tempo the take was played at, when it is not the
score's own. Leave it empty and the score's tempo is used (the placeholder
shows it). A take at ♩= 60 of a score marked ♩= 120 runs at half speed,
and the bars stretch to match.

<Aside type="note" title="The score's tempo is the grid">
battuta does not guess a tempo from the audio. The score says how long a
measure is; the recording is placed on that grid with the two numbers
above. A take with rubato will drift from the bars within a phrase — that
is the take, and seeing it is the point. Measure-by-measure alignment is
planned as a later step.
</Aside>

## On the score

With **on the score** checked, every measure in edit view carries its
slice of the trace, drawn over the staff whose pitch is nearest: the
trace sits exactly on that measure's noteheads, and time runs along the
notes' onsets. The written notes appear as faint bars for their length,
so a note held too short or too long shows as a trace that stops early or
runs on. Uncheck it to see the notation alone; the panel keeps the
recording.

<Shot name="pitch-reference-tiles" caption="The trace over the measures, on the noteheads: where the take was sharp, flat, early or late is visible at the note." />

<Aside type="caution" title="One voice at a time">
The detector reads one pitch at a time, so it wants a voice or a single
instrument. A chord, or two parts recorded together, give a trace that
jumps between them. Record the part you want to check on its own.
</Aside>

## Turning it off

battuta menu → **🌣 shortcuts** → **plugins** → *Pitch reference*. The 🎙,
the panel and the traces go together, and any analysis in progress
stops. Nothing about your score changes — the plugin only ever reads it.
