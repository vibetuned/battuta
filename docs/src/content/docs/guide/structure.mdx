Everything on this page changes the shape of the score rather than its notes. All
of it is one undo step per action, and all of it reverts exactly.

## Measures

<KeymapTable ids={["structural"]} />

The header's **+m**, **−m** and **⧉m** buttons insert, delete and duplicate
measures; the numpad keys do the same thing. Without a selection they act on the
caret's measure; with a block they act on the block's measure range, so a
three-measure block duplicates three measures.

New measures arrive with the right staff shape — every staff, and every *voice*
of their neighbour, filled with whole-measure rests.

**Measure numbers stay sane.** Insert, delete and duplicate renumber the
measures sequentially afterwards; a pickup measure keeps its 0-based numbering,
and editorial numbering that isn't a plain number is never touched. Undo restores
the original numbers exactly.

<Aside type="note" title="Inserts respect context boundaries">
Insert or duplicate next to a mid-score meter or key change and the new measures
stay in the region they came from — a duplicated 4/4 measure never lands inside
the 7/8 section that follows it.
</Aside>

## Staves

The **staves** select in the status bar shows the staff count and offers two
operations:

- ***add staff below*** appends a treble staff, gives every measure a
  whole-measure rest on it, and puts the caret on the new staff's first measure
  so you can start typing straight away.
- ***remove caret staff*** removes the caret's staff from the whole score — its
  definition, any mid-score redefinitions, its content in every measure, and the
  control events anchored to it. The last remaining staff is refused.

## Voices

Voices are per staff and per measure. The **voices** select shows the caret's
voice and offers switch, add and remove.

- ***add a voice*** puts a new voice — whole-measure rests — into the caret's
  staff **from the caret's measure onward**, the same scoping rule clef, key and
  meter changes use. At measure 1 that means the whole score.
- ***remove this voice*** takes it out from the caret's measure on, together with
  the control events anchored to it. A staff's last voice is refused.

<Figure name="voices" caption="Two voices in one staff: Verovio stems voice 1 up and voice 2 down." />

A voice added mid-score draws the engraver's **double barline** at the boundary
where it starts (an existing special barline is left alone).

Note entry works in any voice exactly as it does in voice 1. Where a staff has
more than one voice, battuta tints them so you can see which one you are in:
voice 1 blue, voice 2 violet, voice 3 amber, voice 4 magenta. The caret and
selection colours always win over the tint.

<kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> traverse voices before staves — see
[Navigation](../navigation/#walking-through-the-music).

## Clef, key and meter

The **clef**, **key** and **meter** selects always show the context *in force at
the caret*, and picking a value applies the change *at the caret's measure*.

| Select | Scope | Options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| clef | the caret's staff | treble, bass, alto, tenor, octave-down treble |
| key | score-wide, from the caret's measure | 7♭ through 7♯ |
| meter | score-wide, from the caret's measure | 4/4, 3/4, 2/4, 2/2, 3/2, 6/8, 9/8, 12/8, 5/4, 7/8, 5/8, 3/8 |

<Figure name="clef-change" caption="A mid-staff clef change, written before the barline where an engraver puts it." />
<Figure name="key-meter-change" caption="A key and meter change at a measure start." />

At measure 1, battuta edits the score's initial definitions in place. Mid-score it
inserts the MEI-native form: an interleaved definition for key and meter, and an
**inline clef before the barline** — the engraver's position, and the form that
survives page view and export. Repeated changes at the same place merge instead of
stacking.

Downstream tiles re-render automatically, because a context change moves their
cache key. That is also why a new key or meter becomes visible exactly where it
changes and not before.

<Aside type="caution" title="A meter change can be refused">
A new meter is validated against every measure up to the next meter change. If
one of them no longer fits, battuta refuses and names the first offender —
change the meter before entering notes, or on whole-measure rests, where anything
fits.
</Aside>

## The score title

The **title** button in the header edits the MEI title (`meiHead`). If the file
has no title chain at all, battuta creates one. Page view prints it; the button
relabels itself to the current title, so you can see at a glance what the score
calls itself.
