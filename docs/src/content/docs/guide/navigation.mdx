The caret is battuta's answer to "where am I?". It is not a pixel position but a
place in the document: **measure, staff, voice, event**. The status bar spells it
out — `[ m 10, s 2, v 1, n 6 ]` — and the blue highlight in the score shows the
event it sits on.

## Placing the caret

**Click a note, rest or chord.** Clicking a chord puts the caret on the whole
chord, even if you hit one of its noteheads. Clicking a slur, a barline or a
staff line lands on the closest event in that measure instead, so you rarely
have to aim precisely.

## Walking through the music

<KeymapTable ids={["navigation", "rowNavigation"]} />

**<kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd>** step event by event through the caret's voice
and cross measure boundaries as if the barlines weren't there. They stop at the
voice's own start and end: a second voice that only exists in measures 5–8 does
not teleport you to measure 1.

**<kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd>** move vertically through the slots in the
measure — voice 1, voice 2, then the next staff's voices — and when the slots run
out they continue to the adjacent **row** of the score, entering at its top slot
going down and its bottom slot coming up, landing on the note nearest the
caret's horizontal position. That is exactly how a text cursor behaves at the
end of a line.

**<kbd>Home</kbd> / <kbd>End</kbd>** jump to the first or last event of the
engraved row you are on. **<kbd>PageUp</kbd> / <kbd>PageDown</kbd>** move to the
previous or next row, keeping your staff and voice where the landing measure
has them.

<Aside type="note" title="Rows, not systems">
In edit view battuta flows measure tiles into rows the way a paragraph flows
words, so a "row" is however many measures fit your window at the current zoom.
Resize the window and Home/End cover a different span — the same way rewrapping
text changes what "end of line" means.
</Aside>

### The view follows the caret

When navigation or note entry pushes the caret past the edge of the window, the
score scrolls it back to the middle, smoothly, clearing the sticky header and
the status bar. Re-layout, zoom changes and background tile renders never scroll
the view — only an actual caret move does.

If the caret lands in a measure that hasn't been rendered yet (large scores
render lazily), battuta scrolls to its place, which triggers the render, and the
caret appears as soon as the tile exists.

## Zoom

<kbd>ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>+</kbd> / <kbd>ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>−</kbd> zoom in and out
in 25 % steps from 50 % to 250 %; <kbd>ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>0</kbd> returns to
100 %. The same steps are behind the **zoom** button in the status bar. Your
zoom level is remembered between sessions, and new documents open at it.

The browser's own page zoom is suppressed on these keys — in battuta they always
mean the score.

## Clearing state

<kbd>esc</kbd> drops any selection and any block. In note input mode,
<kbd>esc</kbd> first leaves input mode (press it again to clear a selection).

## Between the two views

The caret is drawn in edit view. Switching to page view keeps the position — it
is stored in the document, not on screen — so you can proofread a passage, come
back, and carry on typing where you were.
